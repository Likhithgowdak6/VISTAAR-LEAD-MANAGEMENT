# Demo script

A 12–15 minute walkthrough for someone who runs a business, not a server. No jargon.

**Before you start:** log in, have one live conversation in the Inbox, have your own phone next to
you with the self-chat open, and have the knowledge base already filled with a few real facts. A
demo on an empty system looks like an empty system.

---

## Open with the problem, not the product (30 seconds)

> "You get an enquiry on WhatsApp at 9pm while you're shooting a wedding. By the time you reply
> next morning, they've messaged four other studios and booked one of them. You didn't lose that
> job on price or on quality. You lost it on eleven hours."
>
> "This answers within a minute, every time, in your words — and tells you the moment something
> needs an actual human."

Don't say "AI-powered CRM". Say what it stops costing them.

---

## 1. The Inbox — "this is your day" (2 min)

Open the Inbox. Click a real conversation.

**Point at three things, in this order:**

1. **The thread.** "The customer messaged. This replied in under a minute. Read it — it sounds like
   the studio, not like a bot."
2. **The summary at the top.** "Come back to a lead after four days and you don't scroll twenty
   messages. It tells you where it stands and what's still open."
3. **The panel on the right.** "What service they want, when the event is, and how warm they are —
   0 to 100. Scroll the inbox and you can see which lead to call first without opening anything."

> **The line that lands:** "You're not reading conversations any more. You're reading a list of who's
> worth your time."

---

## 2. The switch — "you're still in charge" (1 min)

This is the most important 60 seconds of the demo. People are afraid of a bot talking to their
customers.

Point at the **AI toggle** on a conversation row. Flip it off.

> "See that. The AI is now completely silent on this one lead. No replies, no follow-ups, nothing —
> until you turn it back on. Every conversation has that switch."

Then say the rule:

> "And even when it's on, it only asks qualifying questions by itself. Anything that sounds like
> selling — a price, a proposal — waits for you to approve it. It will never quote a number you
> didn't give it."

If they ask *"what if it says something stupid?"* — you've already answered it. That's why this
comes early.

---

## 3. Knowledge — "you teach it, in your own words" (2 min)

Go to **Knowledge**. Click **Tell the AI what to do**.

Type it badly on purpose:

```
always greet the customer by name before answering
```

Click **✦ Optimize the prompt**.

> "I typed that the way I'd say it out loud. It turned it into a proper instruction and filed it
> under Rules — which is the section the AI treats as non-negotiable."

Save it. Then show an existing pricing entry.

> "This is where your prices live. It quotes from here word for word, or it doesn't quote at all.
> It cannot invent a discount to win a job."

---

## 4. Templates — "your quote, four ways" (2 min)

Go to **Templates** → **New price template**. Type real numbers roughly:

```
300 edited photos one day 45k
photo + video one day 85k, includes a reel
```

Click **✦ Write four versions**.

> "Same prices, four ways of saying them. One's a clean list, one leads with what they're getting,
> one's just the number for someone in a hurry, one's warm."

Pick one, save it. Then open a conversation and show the **Template…** dropdown in the reply box.

> "Now it's one click in any chat. And notice — it loads it into the box. It doesn't send it. You
> still press send."

---

## 5. Your phone — the part that sells it (3 min)

Pick up your phone. Open the self-chat.

**a) Ask it something:**

Type: `how many birthday bookings do we have`

Wait for the reply.

> "That's not a guess. It counted. I can ask it anything about my own pipeline from my phone, in
> the middle of a shoot — how many hot leads, who's booked next week, what we're getting most
> enquiries for."

**b) Show a real alert** (scroll up to one you've received):

> "Every new lead pings me here with their name and what they want. If I don't reply within ten
> minutes, it **calls me**. An actual phone call. Because a lead sitting unanswered for four hours
> is the thing that costs money, and a notification I've swiped away doesn't fix that."

**c) Show an escalation, if you have one:**

> "And when a lead pushed on price, it stopped and asked me. It didn't negotiate. Discounts are
> mine to give."

---

## 6. After the booking — "nothing gets forgotten" (2 min)

> "Once a deal's won and I've got the date, it changes job. It stops selling and starts making sure
> I don't drop the ball."

- **Every morning for the week before:** one message listing every shoot coming up, soonest first.
- **The night before:** it calls. Names the clients. Only when there's actually something tomorrow —
  a daily call that usually says 'nothing' gets ignored by Wednesday.
- **The day after:** "Did the payment come in?" Say collected and it goes quiet. Say pending and the
  client gets **one** polite reminder, then it's assigned back to you.

> **Say this one out loud:** "And it will never message a customer about money unless I've said so.
> If I don't reply, it asks me again — it doesn't guess. Asking someone who already paid to pay
> again isn't a bug, it's an insult, and it lands on someone who just gave me money."

---

## 7. Meta ads — "leads from your ads land here too" (1 min)

Show **Lead Sources**.

> "If you run lead ads on Instagram or Facebook, those leads come straight in here — name, number,
> what they asked for — and get treated exactly like a WhatsApp enquiry."

Point at the three switches on a source.

> "Per form: import or not, whether the AI sees the answers, and whether it messages them first.
> That last one's off unless you turn it on, and when it's on it waits five minutes — long enough
> that it doesn't feel like they're being watched, short enough that they still remember filling it
> in."

---

## Close (30 seconds)

> "Nothing here sends a message to your customer without your rules and, for anything that matters,
> your approval. What it does is make sure nobody waits eleven hours, nobody gets forgotten after
> the shoot, and you're never the bottleneck on your own business."

Then stop talking and let them ask.

---

## The five questions you'll get, and the honest answers

**"Will it say something wrong to my customer?"**
> It only sends qualifying questions on its own — when, where, what kind of shoot. Anything that
> sounds like selling waits for you. It can't quote a price that isn't in the system, can't promise
> a date, can't offer a discount. And any conversation can be switched off in one tap.

**"Does it sound like a robot?"**
> Show them an actual reply on screen. Don't argue, just let them read it.

**"What if I want to take over?"**
> Flip the switch on that conversation, or just reply yourself — it notices you've stepped in and
> steps back.

**"What happens if WhatsApp goes down / phone dies?"**
> Nothing is lost. Messages queue and send when it reconnects. Reminders retry until they land —
> a duplicate reminder is annoying, a missing one means a photographer doesn't turn up.

**"Is my customer data safe?"**
> Phone numbers are encrypted and only shown when someone deliberately asks, and that's logged.
> Even the assistant on my phone won't read a customer's number out to me.

---

## What not to do in a demo

- **Don't open an empty inbox.** Seed it first.
- **Don't promise the ad-form auto-message** unless you've actually switched it on and seen it work.
  It's built, but it's off by default and untested against a live form.
- **Don't demo the payment reminder yet.** It's written but not switched on. If they ask, say
  "that's coming" — don't show a screen that isn't running.
- **Don't say "AI" more than you have to.** Say "it replies", "it reminds me", "it calls me".
